# Research the public readers of a policy proposal

Use this workflow when the user asks who may review a complaint, which current council members have relevant duties, or how to adapt a proposal to their published policy work. Produce a dated reader map and reviewable introductory notes. Keep the personal campaign analysis and evidence in ignored complaint storage.

## Verify the current institution first

1. Read the selected proposal, its version and the real delivery record. Preserve their hashes before preparing reader-specific wording.
2. Identify the institution, current council term, responsible committee and implementation office. Use the current official roster and dated minutes. Check a second official role surface when choosing a named reader.
3. Read the professional office's published duties. Open its team details when the first page shows only a chief. Distinguish committee-wide legislative analysts, agenda support and member-specific policy support.
4. Read the actual submission guide and its dated window. A page's information-management department is not proof of the substantive reviewing officer or distribution chain.
5. Resolve stale or incomplete retrieval before assigning a current role. A dated historical speech proves what was said then; it does not retain that person's old committee position.

On 2026-10-09, the [Housing and Space Committee roster](https://www.smc.seoul.kr/comt/index.do?indexId=014) and member homepages identify the 12th council's chair and members. The [Urban Planning and Balance Committee roster](https://www.smc.seoul.kr/comt/index.do?indexId=032) places a former housing-committee speaker in a different current committee. The [professional-office page](https://www.smc.seoul.kr/comt/orgSitPolicy.do?menuId=014001008) provides team responsibilities. These are dated checks, not permanent assignments.

## Separate a role from actual reading

Record these fields separately:

| Field | Required evidence or meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Current public role | Official institution, committee, position, district when relevant, observation date and URL |
| Formal remit | Published jurisdiction or task description; do not infer unilateral rule-changing power |
| Relevant public work | Exact programme, meeting or publication date, speaker and source location |
| Countercontext | Relevant competing priorities or objections in the same original source |
| Proposed reader emphasis | An analytical recommendation, with its reason |
| Delivery channel | Verified official route; an untested contact option remains pending |
| Actual handling | Receipt, assignment, reply or other primary record; otherwise unknown |
| Position on the requested change | An express public position or reply; otherwise unknown |

Use qualitative categories such as primary oversight route, related issue lead and implementation office. Do not turn a relevant speech into a response probability, an endorsement or evidence that the person read the proposal. A public employee directory may omit staff names while publishing duties. Retain the role when the name is not published.

## Read relevant work and countercontext

Start with current committee minutes, members' official publications, bills and review reports. Search results and a homepage headline are discovery leads. Read the original passage and surrounding exchange before classifying relevance. An official member release confirms the member's statement; it does not independently prove every allegation in the release.

Keep different programmes separate. A social-housing deposit-refund question can support a cash-flow or implementation review perspective. It does not establish support for a different programme's income-ceiling abolition. A member concerned about access for cash-rich applicants may prefer lowering deposits, financing changes or another remedy. Record those alternatives as possible objections without replacing the user's selected demand.

Research public duties and public policy work. Do not infer a position from party membership, personal finances, family circumstances or unverified personality descriptions. A current chair can be a necessary oversight recipient while also having strong allocation concerns about the proposal.

## Adapt the introduction and question order

Keep the same selected demand, evidence and assumptions for every reader. Change the short introduction, evidence order and institutional action requested. Do not give different readers incompatible promises.

| Reader function | Useful first material | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Committee legislative analyst | One-page issue summary, matched criteria and source-page map | Which agency must supply the combined-conditions analysis and rule-change position? |
| Committee chair | Public-interest problem and scarce-unit allocation tradeoff | What audit question or written-agency decision is needed? |
| Member with a direct programme question | Dated prior question and new evidence | What unresolved issue does this evidence add to that question? |
| Member examining implementation | Owner, remaining procedures and exact reply gaps | Who can turn a general review statement into a traceable next step? |
| Implementing office | Rule version, calculation assumptions and requested decisions | What can this office change, and what requires another authority? |

Use one formal institutional record as the main route. Prepare a small number of supplementary introductory notes for relevant members. Check whether a recipient has already received the institutional record before proposing another delivery. A prepared note does not prove that a filing occurred or that the institution will distribute it to every named member.

The [2026 audit guide](https://www.smc.seoul.kr/board/BoardList.do?boardTypeId=162&menuId=001005009), read on 2026-10-09, states a 2026-09-04 to 2026-10-23 window and permits some tips to be used only as reference material. Recheck the window and route at transmission time. Do not invent direct member delivery, mandatory adoption or direct mayor reading.

## Prepare research-based roleplay contexts

Load the [official review roleplay](official-roleplay.md). Convert a verified public policy concern into a constructed review lens, such as allocation, programme purpose, funding feasibility, implementation or selection fairness.

Each context needs the source concern, its date, the inference limit and one review question. Use fictional role labels. Do not simulate a real named official's private thoughts, promise their reaction or present invented speech as their statement. A prompt may cite the public concern while asking a generic reviewer to examine it. Preserve the existing proposal and answer items. An objection raised by a practice role does not adopt a new policy condition.

## Preserve and validate the research

Save official-source snapshots with source URL, observed time and hash. Distinguish source, observation, assertion and recommendation. Record failed reads and partial extraction. If a cached parser view differs from a fresh official retrieval, preserve both observations and state how the role was resolved. Do not call a tool access error an empty institutional record.

Verify names and roles against the dated sources, contact provenance, local links and private-data exclusion. Preserve the campaign packet and ledger unless a separate revision or delivery action is authorised. Commit the reusable method and deidentified handoff; keep personal strategy, receipts and raw evidence private.

Record the lessons in [the complaint lessons](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-public-reader-research-on-2026-10-09). A researched reader list does not send a message, start a monitor or change a real response status.
