# Reviewing deposit and income ceiling proposals

Use a savings scenario to compare a programme's deposit and income ceiling. Keep the user's selected policy change first. A reviewed formula is a proposal, not a current admission rule or proof of affordable entry. Store personal examples, reviewer reports and calculations in ignored complaint data.

## Define the quantities

- `D`: the full deposit, in currency units.
- `Y`: annual net household labour income, in the same currency units per year.
- `C`: the programme income ceiling converted to the same annual net household income basis.
- `T`: the number of annual saving periods assumed for the comparison.
- `s`: the assumed share of net income saved each year.

Define net income explicitly, including which taxes and compulsory insurance payments are deducted. A seven-period scenario with saving rates of 30, 40 and 50 percent gives `X = T*s` of 2.1, 2.8 and 3.5 respectively. These are proposed scenario inputs, not observed household saving rates. Constant income, no starting wealth, no transfers, no loan and no investment return are modelling assumptions. A marriage-period eligibility cutoff does not establish a household's actual work or saving history.

## Separate a programme rule from an applicant test

For the benchmark household, accumulated savings are `T*s*Y`. The income needed to save the whole deposit is `Y = D/(T*s)`.

The proposed programme design condition is `D <= T*s*C`. If the institution accepts this benchmark while retaining an income ceiling, the implied minimum ceiling is `C >= D/(T*s)`. One linkage option is `C_new = max(C_base, D/(T*s))`, which retains a higher existing ceiling. Another option is to reduce the deposit while keeping the ceiling. A deposit-linked exemption can avoid setting an extremely high new ceiling; its deposit boundary, applicable supply and legal route need explicit definition.

For a ceiling-linkage proposal, do not silently apply `D <= T*s*Y` as a new applicant requirement. It implies `Y >= D/(T*s)` and creates an income floor. Do not add proof of seven years of savings, no past gifts or earnings origin without an explicit policy choice. Labour income is the benchmark scenario; restricting admission to employees would be a separate policy choice that could exclude self-employed households.

If the user explicitly proposes a historical earned-income floor, review it as a separate intentional option. Earlier preferences against extra tests do not override later instructions. Define the period, household/gross/net basis and applicable supply. A cumulative floor derived from savings is `H >= D/s`, where `H` is net income summed over the defined period, not annual income. It neither proves funds were saved nor excludes family support. Compare exclusion of short-career, interrupted-career and self-employed households with the intended access goal. An earned-income or tax-history route/priority is another policy option, not an established equivalent or current programme entitlement. Keep it a candidate until selected for a packet revision.

For a proposed recognised asset amount, first identify its function. Total assessed wealth for an upper limit and earned-origin own funds for a financing floor are different quantities. Subtracting gifts or investment-derived holdings from assessed wealth can make wealthy recipients more likely to pass the upper limit; excluding those sources from an earned-funds qualification amount serves the opposite purpose. Preserve actual total-wealth assessment separately. If loans and deferral remain permitted, define the own-fund amount as `R = D - permitted_deferral - available_borrowing`; exclude borrowing from earned own funds and assess later debt service separately. A full-deposit earned-funds condition deliberately selects a narrower, self-funded group and may conflict with a separate asset ceiling. Say which option is proposed.

Distinguish verified current funds of defined origin from historical income multiplied by an assumed saving rate. The latter estimates capacity and cannot prove saving, present liquidity or absence of transfers. Define the period and transfer/return categories without labelling every lawful investment as speculation. Income records and account evidence have different roles; no simple income certificate proves a negative lifetime gift history. Compare an exclusive qualification gate with an additional eligibility route or priority, without silently replacing the user's proposed exclusive rule. Record new alternatives separately and preserve the selected packet until changed deliberately. Follow the [earned-funds design lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-earned-funds-recognition-on-2026-10-09).

When the user raises simplification, compare applicant evidence, new definitions, exceptions and the institution's work before adding a fairness test. A fund-origin gate introduces more than a single amount: the lookback period, source tracing, mixed funds and career treatment need decisions. Preserve the selected high-deposit income-ceiling abolition request as the main change. Family-support advantage can motivate evaluating the current conditions without becoming a new parental-wealth or gift-history test. Keep a deposit-linked ceiling as the existing fallback and publish any comparison at programme level. Record a tentative concern as a concern, not rejection or adoption of an alternative. Follow the [simplification lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-the-simplification-follow-up-on-2026-10-09).

## Interpret the saving rate and income growth

When the user requests take-home income, replace the main gross-bound comparison with a separately reproducible net-income scenario. Keep the recruitment assessment ceiling unchanged as the source input. Specify the number of employees, their wage split, taxable allowances, dependants and deductions. Assessment income is not automatically taxable wages; label that conversion as an assumption when the notice does not provide it.

Use current official tax and employee social-insurance sources. Calculate each employee's earned-income deduction, personal deduction, pension and special insurance deductions, progressive income tax, applicable tax credits and local income tax. Subtract actual employee contributions and final tax from wages once. An insurance tax deduction reduces the tax base; it is not another cash expense. Do not add an incompatible standard credit or use an unexplained flat tax discount. State whether the estimate uses annual final tax or monthly withholding, and record billing and rounding approximations.

Show estimated household annual take-home income and its monthly average before the savings table. It is an estimate under the declared wage split and deductions, not an actual payslip or the institution's official net-income ceiling. Use that same annual amount for every funding row. Keep source-backed notice amounts, hypothetical loans and actual eligibility separate. Holding current income and one year's rules constant for seven years is a policy comparison; it does not reconstruct past payroll or predict future tax and insurance rates.

Preserve the earlier gross bound, formulas, reviewer inputs and submitted record. Revise the active body, PDF, calculation file and current pointers together after all-page visual QA. A previously reviewed wording candidate needs a new revision if its numbers change; the old roleplay does not review the new input. Keep it separate from the active packet unless deliberately adopted. Validate nested revision numbers as well as filenames, body limits, hashes and frozen versions. Follow the [net-income lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-the-net-income-table-revision-on-2026-10-10).

State the reader's calculation question before choosing a table. For a fixed verified annual net income ceiling `C`, direct savings are `T*s*C`, so funding scenarios do not change the saved amount. Show that amount once by saving rate, then compare it with each required own-fund amount and funding shortfall. Inverse required income is `own_funds/(T*s)` and differs by funding scenario; label it explicitly as required annual income if shown. A column called annual income alone conceals that distinction. If only an assessment-income annual equivalent `A` is available, label `T*s*A` as an optimistic bound before tax deduction, not verified net savings or a net-income conversion. Retain inverse values for audit rather than silently changing them into direct-savings values. Follow the [table-clarity lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-the-savings-table-clarification-on-2026-10-09).

A 30-percent benchmark assumes less saving capacity and leaves more income for other expenditure than a 50-percent benchmark. It therefore produces a higher minimum income ceiling and a wider income permission range. Calling this a conservative funding assumption does not make it a conservative eligibility restriction. A 40-percent midpoint is a comparison value, not evidence of the correct saving rate. Label any recommended rate as a policy choice and show the sensitivity range.

For a backwards-looking growth scenario, define which annual periods are included. If current net income is the most recent of seven annual amounts, accumulation is `s*Y*sum((1+g)^(-k), k=0..6)`. Positive past growth lowers past income relative to current income and raises the current income required to fund the deposit. Gross salary growth is not automatically net-income growth. Keep this sensitivity outside the simple main rule unless requested.

## Check implementation and remaining constraints

When the user proposes that an asset ceiling can shorten support as earned savings grow, state the mechanism conditionally: assessed net assets must actually rise above the applicable ceiling, and that ceiling must remain effective at renewal. High income alone does not establish saving, threshold crossing or a shorter stay. Distinguish initial admission from renewal, and inspect programme-specific childbirth exemptions before using an automatic-exit argument. A periodic-asset-assessment design is a candidate, not an adopted request to end childbirth incentives or force departure. Preserve the selected deposit-linked asset adjustment and record the user's rationale without silently revising a frozen packet. Follow the [asset-exit lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-the-asset-exit-rationale-on-2026-10-09).

Review source-of-funds fairness without claiming that all gifts or investment returns escape assessment. Assets retained as deposits, securities or other assessed holdings can affect eligibility. Separate income assessment, asset assessment, tax liability, household scope and timing. An asset type absent from an enumerated list does not by itself prove complete exclusion from actual screening. Record the exact rule version and ask for the applicable cryptocurrency treatment. Another programme's tax-history criterion is a comparative precedent, not authority to import it. See the [dated fairness lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-earned-income-fairness-review-on-2026-10-09).

Keep gross and net income ceilings separate. A gross ceiling cannot be inserted directly into this net-income formula. Require a published standard conversion by the relevant household/recruitment class, without inventing a flat tax rate. Record the calculation convention, applicable announcement and basis date.

If all of the saved deposit is included in assessed assets at the relevant date, with no debt deduction or exemption, a deposit above the asset ceiling excludes that fully self-funded scenario. This conditional result does not prove the whole eligible population is empty. Verify cash, lease-deposit, loan and instalment treatment in the matched notice. An income exception does not automatically change the asset rule or finance today's deposit.

When the user later proposes deposit-linked asset limits, reopen that policy question instead of treating an earlier asset-preservation choice as permanent. Distinguish a current rule from a proposed change and an unselected numerical formula. Link to the rental deposit, not the purchase value. For a matched household, recognised assets minus recognised debts determine assessed wealth; available cash and payment deferral are separate quantities. If the full self-funded deposit is assessed and exceeds the ceiling, that debt-free scenario fails the asset test. A smaller own-fund amount plus a qualifying loan can produce a different assessed-wealth result, but availability and treatment must be verified. Borrowing itself does not erase existing net wealth. Explain this conditional result without declaring all admission impossible or every loan-supported household eligible. Review income and asset changes together, while keeping parental/gift-history tests outside scope. Raising a ceiling broadens the wealth range; describe that distributive choice rather than asserting that a deposit-linked limit eliminates family-resource advantages. Preserve the frozen packet until a specific revision is selected. Follow the [asset-linkage lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-the-deposit-linked-asset-review-on-2026-10-09).

Check ordinary rules and special supply routes together. Article 18 distinguishes income-setting rules by unit area, and Article 23(2) provides a conditional special supply route that can depart from Article 18. The existence of that route does not establish its application to the programme or authority for this specific change. Ask the institution for the actual procedure, consultation/approval basis and implementation route. Sources: [current rule, effective 2026-08-24](https://law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lspttninfSeq=141321); [city programme summary, modified 2026-08-31](https://news.seoul.go.kr/citybuild/archives/525640).

## Read the matched notice before declaring an omission

Give the documented treatment first, then identify the remaining uncertainty by asset or income category. A gift or inheritance is not listed as a separate income item in the reviewed notice. Received funds retained as deposits, securities or real estate are assessed under the corresponding asset category. Interest and dividends are listed as income. Securities and bank balances are assessed assets. These facts do not establish identical treatment of occasional trading gains, cryptocurrency holdings or every transfer arrangement. Request the applicable classification, valuation date, data source and treatment of an unqueried holding for each unresolved category. Do not infer complete exclusion from an absent name or failed search.

Verify the household definition before describing parental-wealth screening. The reviewed notice includes certain parents who share the applicant's or separated spouse's resident-registration household. It does not establish universal screening of parents living in a separate household. Neither a parent's wealth nor a gift amount is automatically the applicant's annual labour income.

## Compare today's funding with accumulated earnings

State whether the amount is a purchase price or a rental deposit. Today's funding equation is `D = usable own funds + available bank borrowing + permitted payment deferral`. Assessed total assets, after recognised debt deductions, are a different quantity from usable cash. A repayment or interest burden also remains after the upfront equation balances. A hypothetical loan amount is not evidence that a particular applicant, unit or bank product qualifies.

For an illustrative KRW 1 billion deposit, a 30-percent deferral leaves KRW 700 million payable. A further assumed KRW 300 million bank loan leaves KRW 400 million of own funds. Saving that own-fund amount from zero over seven equal annual periods requires net household income of approximately KRW 190.48, 142.86 or 114.29 million at 30, 40 or 50 percent saving rates. Saving the full deposit without a loan or deferral instead requires approximately KRW 476.19, 357.14 or 285.71 million. These are derived scenarios, not current admission thresholds or personal loan advice.

Match every ceiling to the same notice, unit area, household size and child-related uplift. In the reviewed eighth notice, a two-person working couple applying for a unit of 60 square metres or less has a monthly assessment-income ceiling of KRW 10,559,286, equivalent to KRW 126,711,432 over twelve months. Do not label that annual equivalent as net spendable income. The basic assessed-asset ceiling is KRW 662 million; qualifying child categories have higher limits. The notice permits a 30-percent initial-deposit deferral, with a 2026 interest rate of 2.73 percent. These figures come from printed pages 5, 13, 16–18 of the notice linked above.

Use an optimistic bound when no verified net-income conversion is available: treating the entire annual assessment ceiling as spendable income, seven years at 50 percent produces KRW 443,490,012. Under those stated assumptions it cannot produce the KRW 700 million needed without bank borrowing. This is a finding about the modelled household, not proof that every admitted household is unable to finance the unit. Earlier savings, different household classes, debt, source timing and other eligibility conditions remain relevant.

For a fully self-funded, debt-free household, compare required own funds with the applicable asset ceiling only when both use the relevant valuation and eligibility dates. Verify how the deferred portion enters contract-deposit valuation and recognised liabilities before claiming that the KRW 400 million own-fund scenario satisfies the asset limit. The notice uses social-security-system query results, and eligibility must continue through entry. Do not suggest a later transfer as a way to avoid assessment. Ask the institution to publish a standard household funding table that satisfies the deposit, income ceiling, asset ceiling and actual financing conditions together. Separate information about held analyses from a request to create a new analysis.

## Review and prepare a proposal

For a recipient-capture evidence question, search for matched contracted or admitted households and their deposit funding sources. Payment-deferral uptake measures use of that payment option; it does not measure parental assistance. A council member's concern is evidence that the concern was raised, not a measured recipient distribution. General housing-transfer research uses a different population and cannot establish this programme's beneficiaries. Report a scoped unsuccessful search as unconfirmed availability, not proof that no records exist. Ask separately for held survey instruments, funding-source results and anonymised existing tabulations; keep a request for new analysis distinct. Do not turn that research question into an additional applicant screening rule. Follow the [recipient-evidence lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-the-recipient-evidence-search-on-2026-10-09).

When the user asks for a coherent argument from the conversation, reconstruct its premises before adding evidence or demands. For the selected high-deposit income exception, connect the access purpose, asset formation through earnings, the required deposit, borrowing costs and the income ceiling. Present one conclusion: the institution should explain a feasible earned-income route under the combined conditions, or change the selected deposit-linked ceiling. A financing table supports that argument; it must not replace it with a growing list of requests.

Do not treat borrowing as a completed affordability solution because the upfront funding equation balances. Verify pre-entry accumulation separately from post-entry cash flow. Post-entry net income must cover essential expenditure, interest, any scheduled principal payments and the assumed cash savings. Borrowing reduces required upfront own funds but interest reduces income available for other uses. Do not deduct post-entry interest from earlier saving periods unless the loan existed then. Apply the income ceiling at its actual eligibility/review stages rather than assuming it persists unchanged forever. An exact feasible path and the actual incidence of family support remain evidence questions.

For a Socratic reconstruction, use short linked questions that test the institution's stated purpose and the consistency of its conditions. Identify the dialogue as a reconstruction, not a historical quotation or a real agency exchange. A question about whether the rules favour access through pre-existing wealth is a hypothesis to test, not proof that gifts escape assessment or that all low-income entrants receive family funds. Keep additional asset-origin tests and unrelated policy demands outside the selected argument.

Adapt the tone when the user rejects personal pleading or repeated confrontational questions. Lead with the programme's verified stated purpose, explain the potential funding-path difference and state the selected improvement constructively. The access concern is possible advantage from available family resources versus accumulation through earned income; it is not established capture by all low-income beneficiaries. Separate rule-level exclusion from the actual composition of admitted households. Retain income/asset screening constraints and describe any missing recipient funding-source evidence narrowly. Do not soften the selected abolition request into an unrelated applicant test, change the frozen packet automatically, or replace the concern with generic affordability language. Follow the [tone and resource-path lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-tone-and-resource-path-framing-on-2026-10-09).

When a user distinguishes current assessed assets from family funds that may become available later, state that distinction first. A personal asset ceiling does not by itself measure access to external family resources. Do not use that ceiling as a complete rebuttal of the funding-path concern. Separately verify continued eligibility and the treatment of later gifts, loans and deposits before stating that post-selection funding is permitted. Do not recommend transfer timing to avoid assessment. An unresolved timing rule does not eliminate the structural access question, and a possible access advantage does not establish actual beneficiary capture. Follow the [latent-family-funding correction](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-the-latent-family-funding-clarification-on-2026-10-09).

For a sharing request, prepare a short public article and a separate evidence note around the selected argument. Lead with the deposit, earned-savings, interest and income-ceiling question. Summarise a supplied call transcript with attribution and time locations. Preserve context such as acknowledgement of the issue, proposed review and the closing response about a written reply. An automated transcript without audio comparison supports an attributed paraphrase; do not present its wording as an audio-verified quotation or an official institutional finding. Keep held-analysis questions distinct from allegations about motives or legal violations. Use dated notices for policy facts and apply the [sharing evidence procedure](sharing-evidence.md).

When the user explains the saving period as a policy-design benchmark for early-career asset formation, lead with that objective. Do not answer as if the user had claimed that every newlywed has seven years of savings. Keep the empirical caveat, but place it after the positive demand for practical access through earned savings. Separate current income from accumulated wealth, and compare the deposit and ceiling together. Use social mix as a supporting proposed access principle, with the official meaning and date explicit; do not present an existing tenure-mixing policy as legal authority to remove income ceilings. Treat family-support advantage as a mechanism to evaluate unless admitted-household evidence establishes its prevalence. Follow the [dated framing lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-the-early-career-policy-framing-on-2026-10-09).

When the user identifies high-earned-income households with little starting wealth, no family support and parental-support outflows as the represented group, preserve those dimensions separately. Current salary, accumulated wealth, access to family funds and the burden of supporting relatives are not interchangeable. Treat supplied personal experience as user-reported context and the fairness principle as a proposed policy standard. Do not convert tax burden into a new entitlement proportional to taxes, introduce parental-support evidence tests, or claim that every high-income household has low means. Scope exclusion/capture claims to the specified programme and require recipient evidence for actual prevalence. Do not present an unmeasured asset-price-versus-wage trend as a verified time series. Existing saving-rate scenarios do not automatically quantify parental support; define cash-flow categories before deducting it and avoid double counting. Record the rationale separately from a frozen packet until a document revision is selected. Follow the [family-support rationale lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-the-family-support-rationale-on-2026-10-09).

For a requested document update adopting the combined income/asset direction, use the [campaign scope-update procedure](official-campaign.md#fix-the-request-and-answer-items). Preserve an institutionally readable purpose/mechanism/recommendation structure, synchronise current policy records and prepare a new versioned envelope after rendering. Keep the asset self-funding comparison conditional on assessment, deductions and timing. Separate unsubmitted new components from the already accepted case.

For an authorised delegated review, give distinct reviewers arithmetic, policy design and counterargument tasks. Recompute numerical examples independently and ask for a review of the combined recommendation. Preserve differences in proposed saving rates and unresolved definitions. Do not count mathematical agreement as evidence that a policy is optimal or a legal route available.

When converting the reviewed argument into an official action plan, use the [official campaign procedure](official-campaign.md). Fix three written answer items, preserve one primary case, check dated council opportunities and treat mayor DMs as optional routing. Distinguish a plan or draft from actual delivery and a written decision from a changed recruitment notice.

Present the chosen rule, sensitivity examples, the prohibition on a new income floor, and the remaining asset/gross-net conditions. Keep optional growth and loan scenarios outside the core demand. Save the reviewed proposal as a candidate until a submission revision is selected. Follow [packet revision procedures](computer-use.md) and [dated lessons](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-the-deposit-and-income-linkage-review-on-2026-10-09).

## Review deposits, financing and the need for support together

For a deposit-by-deposit comparison, define two separate constraints: usable cash before entry and continuing housing cashflow after entry. Use `own_funds = full_deposit - approved_borrowing - permitted_deferral`. A published maximum gives a conditional lower bound for that product, not a verified applicant loan or the minimum across the whole banking market. Check the contract payment required before loan execution. Do not add that payment to own funds twice, or count undisbursed lending as cash already available at signing.

Check every financing path against its own full-contract deposit limit, income and asset rules, household class, unit area, landlord, collateral, guarantee, existing debt and payment dates. Deferring part of the deposit does not by itself reduce the full deposit used for a product's eligibility limit. Verify a matched public-rental agreement before importing private-lease terms. A guarantee ceiling is not a bank loan ceiling. Public-rental exclusions in a local interest subsidy do not establish that all national fund loans exclude public rentals. Keep policy loans, bank loans and deferral separate; do not add published maxima without an explicit compatible route.

Identify dated display rates separately from the execution quote. Use a declared rate assumption and sensitivity when the actual quote is unavailable. Label a high-deposit bank/deferral combination unverified when unit acceptance, landlord consent, guarantee and simultaneous use are not confirmed. Omit ineligible product rows rather than showing a zero loan as an eligible option. Reject hypothetical borrowing above the deposit payable at entry instead of silently producing negative own funds. Record conflicting official guidance and apply only the conditions actually supported; a general fund page cannot resolve every specific product term.

For a bullet-repayment comparison, monthly financing burden is loan interest plus deferral interest. Show any required principal instalment separately if the actual product amortises. Deferral is not a grant. Deposit return, loan maturity, renewal, interest changes and possible deposit increases still matter. Define monthly residual as take-home income minus the specified housing financing payments. State that it is before management, utilities, fees, living costs, childcare, family support and other debt payments unless those inputs were included explicitly. Do not call that amount disposable surplus or an affordable living standard. Use a compatible household income for each product; a policy-loan row cannot use a benchmark household whose income exceeds that product's cap.

A household can face a large entry-cash barrier and a manageable monthly payment, or the reverse. A savings comparison measures the first barrier under declared assumptions; it does not prove every admitted household is unable to fund entry. Reconcile the bank approval sensitivity before using the result as a policy claim. Entry cash and assessed net assets require separate valuation, debt treatment and dates. Do not infer asset eligibility from the funding equation.

Review support need against the stated purpose, actual beneficiary means, additional outcome created by support, public net cost and competing uses of scarce housing or funds. A deposit threshold or ability to pay does not alone establish that support is unnecessary. Product cutoffs are financing boundaries, not empirical wealth thresholds. Ask what support an already well-funded household needs and why that household receives priority, without inventing a beneficiary distribution or family-transfer prevalence. Keep the user's selected access reform intact unless a revision is explicitly adopted.

Other policies can use the same sequence: purpose, formal eligibility, initial payment, continuing cost and risk, actual financing, actual uptake, public outcome, and allocation alternatives. Change the financial model to match the policy. A purchase programme needs transaction costs and amortising repayments; a business grant needs matching funds and operating costs. Do not copy the same income-ceiling repeal or seven-year savings benchmark into every programme. Separate a request for existing analyses or aggregated records from a request to create a new analysis. Follow the [deposit-affordability lesson](lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-the-deposit-affordability-analysis-on-2026-10-10).
