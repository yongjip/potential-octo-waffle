# Policy proposal writing

More-specific applicable context takes precedence for working conventions; levels to the left take priority.

## Reasoning and drafting style

Apply this style to policy analysis and proposals. It records working preferences, not a personality profile or political ideology.

### Build the argument

- Start from the policy's verified purpose. Identify the concrete mismatch between that purpose, access conditions and the resulting support. Compliance with separate rules does not establish that their combined design serves the purpose.
- Follow the user's developing argument before adding another rationale. Keep the comparison household, qualifying conditions, time and policy stage explicit. Answer the question within that scope.
- Preserve the complete policy-design argument while verifying its claims. Use policy purpose → effective access and funding conditions → all relevant support components at their proper time → distributional justification → requested correction. Restrict only a claim that lacks evidence; do not narrow the whole question to a personal investment return or the smallest available estimate. Expand the supported analysis, not unsupported numbers. Keep recurring deposit/financing benefits and a future purchase discount distinct to prevent double counting.
- Distinguish current earned income, accumulated assets and spendable funds. Separate earned savings, gifts and repayable family borrowing. Do not treat high income as proof of existing wealth or access to family support.
- Trace the money: what must the household pay, what support can it receive, and under which conditions? Express recurring benefits annually and future purchase discounts at the exercise date. Use the monetary methods below without replacing nonmonetary outcomes.
- Compare support for the same qualifying conditions. Identify when a house price or an initial choice changes the award size. Ask whether that difference serves the stated purpose. Prefer simple, transparent rules that make the selected benefit predictable when they fit the purpose. A fixed award can equalise one component; it does not equalise every household outcome.
- Determine actual access from the intersection of eligibility, allocation priority, required payments, payment dates and verified financing conditions. Missing recipient microdata does not prevent proving a structural funding barrier or identifying the resource group able to exercise an award.
- Keep structurally necessary resources separate from observed funding sources and measured behavioural effects. A requirement to mobilise several hundred million won can restrict effective access to resource-capable households without proving what share received parental gifts. Define the group and conclusion precisely; do not use an unknown gift share to dilute a demonstrated resource barrier.
- Assess three questions separately: whom the rules favour, who can meet their funding conditions, and who actually received support. Published rules and verified payment/financing conditions can answer the first two within their scope. Recipient data is needed for the third. Missing recipient data must not invalidate an established structural advantage; structural advantage must not be presented as a measured recipient share.
- Distinguish total assets, financial assets and real-estate assets. A real-estate ceiling does not necessarily exclude a household with substantial cash. Keep initial payments, cumulative payments outside a designated loan and confirmed own capital separate. See the [structural-access lesson](../lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-correcting-structural-access-analysis-on-2026-10-10).
- When income determines priority, examine whether the asset definition reflects actual economic resources. Low assessment income is not evidence of low wealth. Separate financial-asset holdings from interest, dividends and other income produced by those holdings. Including a yield in income does not assess the whole stock of assets. A synthetic comparison must include every income component the selected assessment actually counts.
- Compare like-qualified households before evaluating resource-based priority. Hold the other household, marriage, child, residence, debt and supply conditions explicit. Do not treat an illustrative wealthy household as an observed recipient or infer its asset source. Ask for the rule's purpose, justification and correction without prescribing a new parental, gift-history or minimum-income test.
- Apply the general income-versus-wealth principle through each programme's actual rules. Private special supply's real-estate criterion and a public rental's existing total-asset assessment present different questions. Keep financial-asset coverage and priority questions separate from a high-deposit rental's combined income, asset and payment constraints. See the [adopted-logic review lesson](../lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-applying-the-adopted-financial-assets-logic-on-2026-10-10).
- Keep the selected change and fallback visible as the analysis expands. Do not add parental-wealth, gift-history or minimum-income screening merely to solve a comparison problem.

### Write for an official reader

- Name outgoing files for their subject and purpose in the recipient's language. Use short names. Keep internal task labels, revision numbers, agent names and review-status wording out of filenames, covers and footers. Record revision history and hashes in the private manifest. Keep established code identifiers and historical evidence intact. A renamed file needs updated links; a presentation edit also needs a new attachment hash and visual checks.
- Lead with the main point. Use short sentences and concrete terms. For a proposal, use a short purpose-based problem statement, matched evidence or calculation, then a specific correction and requested decision.
- Use a firm, constructive tone. Describe the design issue and public purpose without pleading, accusing a recipient group or turning every sentence into a challenge. Frame the request as a general rule improvement.
- Keep assumptions that change the conclusion beside the relevant calculation. Place internal verification logs and secondary qualifications in supporting records. Do not let unrelated caveats obscure a valid comparison.
- Before proposing a recipient statistic as a next check, identify a dataset that measures the target population and the relevant variable, and verify its access conditions. Agency collection does not establish public access. General-household survey findings do not establish one project's special-supply buyer shares. Do not repeat an unavailable funding-source disclaimer in a proposal that makes no funding-source percentage claim; preserve that audit limit internally.
- Use questions when they reveal a precise design contradiction or elicit a written decision. Follow them with the relevant facts and proposed remedy.
- Correct a mistaken comparison plainly. Do not agree reflexively or defend an earlier answer by changing the question's scope.

At acquisition-time appraisal `V` and a fixed qualifying deduction `C`, purchase price is `V - C` and the gross purchase discount is `C`. Compare that acquisition-stage discount before discussing later ownership returns. Prior appreciation during tenancy is not the tenant's ownership return. See the [purchase-stage lesson](../lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-fixed-awards-and-the-purchase-stage-on-2026-10-10).

## Working conventions

- Show a recipient's annual monetary benefit when a defensible calculation is possible. Use KRW per household per year. State the comparison year and period of eligibility.
- Show the additional gross wage needed to obtain an equivalent benefit when tax treatment and the comparison assumptions permit it. Compare a cost-saving benefit with take-home wages before making a gross-wage comparison.
- Also show the initial own funds, ongoing payments and other access conditions. If financing is unverified, show the entry payment required rather than calling it minimum own cash. A valuable benefit can still be inaccessible to the intended household.
- Use total deposit, initial payment, own capital, bank loan and deferred principal as separate terms. For 30% deferral on KRW 500 million, the initial payment is KRW 350 million and deferred principal KRW 150 million. The initial payment can include own capital and approved borrowing. Do not call it common entry capital, assume the household owns it, or hide it through cancellation in a recipient-facing table. Explain the payment structure before any annual-interest difference; an incremental-cost table is not a total-cost table.
- Compare the same household, service quality, location and period where possible. Keep differences visible when they cannot be held constant.
- Distinguish recipient benefit, government fiscal cost and wider social outcomes. These are separate quantities.
- Label observed values, calculated estimates and illustrative assumptions. Use ranges when the result depends materially on a rate or uncertain input.
- For a submission, select a rate from dated primary evidence before calculating annual benefits. Prefer the household's verified contract rate. If that is unavailable, use an actual new-business average for the relevant product and state the observed period, publication date and population. Do not replace a missing quote with an arbitrary convenient rate. A published average is a reference, not a household loan approval.
- Match the rate to the money being valued. For own funds, use an appropriate deposit return after applicable tax. For borrowed funds, apply the borrowing rate only to the amount actually financed or replaceable by the policy. A single rate is appropriate only where the funding method is the same. Do not average bank rates without volume weights, use an advertised minimum for every household, or apply a lending rate to all self-funded principal.
- Fix the user-requested financing comparison before selecting rates. If the question is borrowing cost, compare interest for equal-own-capital alternatives and use the borrowing rate in both loan-replacement components. Do not switch to a deposit opportunity-return model merely because a deposit statistic is available. Present an own-fund scenario only when requested or useful as a clearly separate comparison. Verify lending limits before calling a conditional estimate an actual household saving.
- Do not assume that all policy support is tax-free or that a noncash benefit is as flexible as cash. Verify the relevant tax treatment. Value a service against the expenditure it actually replaces or clearly label an imputed comparison.
- Preserve safety, rights, accessibility and other outcomes that cannot be reliably priced. Do not force every policy into a single monetary score.

## Monetary comparison methods

These formulas specify comparison methods. They do not establish a programme's current rates, recipients or actual benefits.

| Comparison | Calculation and scope | Required conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual cash benefit | Payments received during the year, less tax on those payments and incremental compulsory participation costs | Match eligibility, payment dates and tax treatment. Loan principal and refundable deposits are not gifted income. |
| Annual expense reduction | Comparable annual expenditure without support minus expenditure with support, including incremental compulsory costs | Use the same service and household. Distinguish observed savings from a hypothetical alternative. |
| Rental-deposit price advantage | `(B - D) × r` for one full year | `B`: matched private-market deposit. `D`: public deposit. `r`: explicitly chosen annual cost-of-funds comparison rate. A lower refundable deposit saves funding or opportunity cost; its full difference is not an annual payment. |
| Additional deferral advantage | `d × (r - i)` for one full year | `d`: deferred part of `D`. `i`: annual deferral rate. Choose `r` for the funds this deferral actually replaces. Use the deposit comparison's `r` only where the funding method matches. Do not add the full deferred principal as a yearly benefit. |
| Single-rate combined deposit advantage | `(B - D) × r + d × (r - i)` | When the same funding method applies to both components, this equals `B × r - (D - d) × r - d × i`. For mixed methods, value each verified funding component separately. Subtract additional annual costs once. Do not count the deposit discount or deferral twice. |
| Additional gross-wage equivalent | `A ÷ (1 - m)` under a constant marginal deduction assumption | `A`: annual benefit already expressed as an after-tax equivalent. `m`: the share deducted from additional wages. Use marginal deductions, not the household's average tax rate. With changing brackets, deductions or insurance caps, solve using the household tax model. |
| Effect of crossing an income threshold | Change in take-home wages plus change in annual net policy benefit | Evaluate matched households just below and above the threshold. Annual benefit already includes incremental participation costs; do not subtract those again. |

For partial-year use, annualise only when the duration and continuing conditions support it. Do not multiply a conditional annual estimate by a maximum residence period as if receipt were guaranteed. Evaluate later rates and conditions separately. Use a stated discount rate if comparing present values over several years.

The wage-equivalent calculation describes additional wages needed to match a benefit. It is not the recipient's observed annual salary, an eligibility-income value or a recommendation for a new income test.

Before treating a funding-cost estimate as an after-tax benefit, adjust for relevant tax on alternative returns or deductions on borrowing costs where applicable. A nominal comparison rate alone does not establish a household's net saving.

## Output order for detailed comparisons

For a short policy memo, use problem → matched evidence or calculation → concrete correction and requested decision. Use the sequence below for detailed analysis or an attachment. Include only the fields needed in a short answer.

1. State the policy purpose and the household used for comparison.
2. Present annual recipient benefit, with the principal components.
3. Present the additional gross-wage equivalent, or explain why it remains unverified.
4. Present entry funds and ongoing affordability.
5. Compare recipients, public costs and measured outcomes against the policy purpose.
6. Identify the proposed change, evidence needed and next decision.

Use a compact table: `policy or amount band | annual recipient benefit | additional gross-wage equivalent | initial funds | ongoing payments | evidence and assumptions`. Omit an inapplicable column rather than inventing a number. Keep observed distributions separate from a synthetic household comparison.

## Illustrative check

Anchor the current housing comparison in the actual notice's Cheongdam Le El 59.78-square-metre deposit: `D = 1,170,000,000` KRW. A 30% deferral gives `d = 351,000,000` and initial total payment `819,000,000`. A comparison at exactly 80% implies `B = 1,462,500,000`; that is an imputed private deposit, not an observed transaction. At the BOK August 2026 actual-statistic loan rate `r = 4.35%` and published 2026 deferral rate `i = 2.73%`, the annual smaller-deposit interest difference is KRW 12,723,750 and the deferral-rate difference is KRW 5,686,200. Total annual difference is KRW 18,409,950, or KRW 1,534,162.5 monthly, conditional on equal own capital and obtainable remaining funding. Show complete household borrowing and deferral interest separately; the benefit difference is not the household's entire bill. Prefer verified contract rates and approved amounts for an actual case. The notice plans entry in April 2027; neither cited 2026 rate establishes the 2027 bill. See the [actual-unit correction](../lessons-learned.md#lessons-from-rebasing-the-housing-comparison-on-2026-10-12).

Use actual units before illustrative amount bands. Do not replace the high-priced unit under discussion with a smaller arbitrary worked example. Keep the same deposit, household and saving assumptions through entry funding, full monthly interest and annual support. A 100% saving row is an arithmetic bound, not an eligible or livable household: review its accumulated assets against the applicable asset ceiling. Preserve already accepted artifacts and prepare corrections as new evidence. Do not rebase a future purchase discount on today's rental deposit; its base is the exercise-stage sale appraisal.

If a policy actually saves a household KRW 10,000,000 a year and creates no additional tax, a hypothetical 30% deduction from additional wages gives `10,000,000 ÷ 0.70 = 14,285,714` KRW of additional gross wages. The 30% is a comparison assumption, not a verified rate for the user or all households.

For a self-funded deposit comparison, suppose `B = 625,000,000`, `D = 500,000,000` and `d = 150,000,000`. The Bank of Korea's actual new-business one-year deposit average for August 2026 was 3.39%. With ordinary 15.4% withholding and no additional household-specific tax, `r = 3.39% × (1 - 15.4%) = 2.86794%`. Using the published 2026 deferral rate `i = 2.73%`, the annual price advantage is KRW 3,584,925 and the additional deferral advantage is KRW 206,910. The combined reference value is KRW 3,791,835. The exact 80% market ratio remains illustrative; this does not confirm a unit's matched market price or the household's actual alternative return.

The same dated source reports an actual new-business rental-deposit loan average of 4.35%. If deferral replaces a verified borrowing amount, the nominal annual interest difference against 2.73% is KRW 1,620,000 per KRW 100 million replaced. Do not apply that rate to the entire deposit without verifying the lending amount, product eligibility, fees and any relevant tax effects.
